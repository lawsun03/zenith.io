## Two Dominant Failure Modes

**1. Stopped_then_target (45% of tracked trades).** Nine trades had the right direction but the stop was hit before price reached target. The mechanism: market-order slippage on MNQ longs tightens the effective stop by 7–43 points. A fill 43pt above the planned entry on a tight stop is effectively a handicapped trade from the start. Limit entries would eliminate this class of failure — the entry price is fixed, so the stop isn't tightened.

**2. Grade floor lowered — all F/D/C trades.** The prior session (06-08) had 12/12 A− trades and lost $720. Today has 0 A/A+/A− trades and is positive. This inverts the expected relationship — the grader's ability to predict outcome at current scoring thresholds is unclear. A new "gapping sack" filter (overlapping same-side FVGs) is doing useful work but the underlying grade floor is very permissive.

## Prioritized Actions

1. **Investigate the grade floor change.** Was this intentional (A/B test)? The result today was positive but 06-08 all-A− was negative — this needs more sessions to interpret. If it's an A/B test, tag it explicitly.

2. **Limit entry mode for MNQ.** The slippage pattern (+43pt, +16pt, +13.25pt on longs) is the primary driver of `stopped_then_target`. A limit entry at the FVG edge fixes the entry price and preserves the stop distance. Highest-leverage mechanical fix.

3. **Cap contract size when slippage is large.** The 13ct MNQ long at +43pt slippage placed 13 contracts with a stop that was already breached. A simple guard: if `|fill - signal_entry| > 0.5 × stop_distance`, reduce size or reject.

4. **Don't adjust the grade floor yet.** One profitable day with F/D/C grades doesn't validate the floor. Track another 2–3 sessions before drawing conclusions.
