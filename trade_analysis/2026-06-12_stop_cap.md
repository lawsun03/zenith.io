# max_stop_atr stop-width cap — rejected (2026-06-12)

Motivated by the live 08:00 PT trade: ssl-30 anchored a 293-pt stop →
r3.5 target 1,026 pts away = a top-1% daily-range ask (5y data: only
1.0% of days range ≥1,026 pts; 2.9% in 2025–26). Lawrence asked for a
cap; built ATR-relative (`max_stop_atr`, fixed-point caps are the
2022–23 drought mechanism) with a structural fallback: swing anchor too
wide → use the sweep-extreme anchor; still too wide → skip.

| variant | test 17mo | 2024 | PF (t/24) | MLL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| control | **6 (35%)** | **4 (33%)** | 1.31 / 1.37 | 0 |
| cap 6×ATR | 4 (24%) | 3 (25%) | 1.32 / 1.35 | 0 |
| cap 4×ATR | 3 (18%) | 3 (25%) | 1.29 / 1.30 | **1** |

REJECT — same lesson as ssl15 (18%) from a new angle: the wide
swing-anchored stops are load-bearing. The trades whose stops the cap
tightened are exactly the high-volatility entries that produce the
pass months; tightening converts their noise into stop-outs. PF flat =
the cap trades neither better nor worse per dollar, it just shrinks
the winners that pay for the month.

The "unrealistic target" itself is mostly cosmetic: targets are rarely
the exit (flatten harvests the runner at 15:05 CT; the 1-lot BE-move at
1.5R kills the risk first). The data-driven fix for exit geometry
remains the MFE/MAE excursion ladder (monitoring backlog #1) — pick
target/trail levels from measured excursion distributions instead of
fixed R on a structural stop. `max_stop_atr` stays in code default-off.

Field validated live the same morning: the open trade (entry 29633,
stop 29339.75) would have become stop ~29483 / target ~30158 under a
6×ATR cap — and the backtest says that geometry earns less over a
month, not more.
